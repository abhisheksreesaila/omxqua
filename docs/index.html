The belief

Two schools. One taste.

Google gets texture right. Apple gets motion right. We take what each does best, say no to the rest, and add what we think is missing.

From Google

Texture you can trust.

Material's surfaces: solid, tonal, one soft shadow. You always know what floats and what sits.

  • Solid surfaces, one shadow scale
  • State tints on hover and press
  • The label that floats into the border
  • Ripples and halos
  • Filled and underlined fields
material 3
From Apple

Motion with weight.

Springs instead of curves. Things squash under your finger and settle like objects do.

  • Real springs, not ease-in-out
  • A squash on every press
  • A switch that leans as you hold it
  • Glass on everything
  • Their fonts, icons and names
human interface guidelines
Ours

The parts neither school has, and one rule that keeps the rest calm.

  • The Beacon: what's live, up top
  • A tab highlight that moves like liquid
  • A logo that writes itself
  • The more often it happens, the less it moves

The little things

Every touch, a tiny joy. Each one has a reason.

These are the real parts, live. Press them. Under each, why it works: research when a study backs it, taste when it's simply what we think feels right.

press and hold each one

Everything presses back.

Every button, chip, tile and logo squashes under your finger and springs home. Small things squash more, big things less, so it feels the same at every size.

researchAn answer within 100 ms feels instant, so the squash starts on touch, before the click. Nielsen; Miller 1968
tasteThe squash itself, and how much it scales with size.
hold it, then let go

The switch leans.

Hold it and the knob stretches toward where it's going, then settles.

tasteFrom Apple. No halo, no ripple.
hold the bar and slide along it

The highlight is liquid.

Tap a tab and the highlight stretches on its way there. Hold the bar and slide: it lifts, follows your thumb and drops where you let go.

researchLabels stay on the tabs: icons alone get misread. They only shrink to icons while you scroll down. NN/g
researchAt the bottom, where the thumb already is. Hoober 2013
tasteThe stretch and the drag are ours.
click into the empty one

The label never leaves.

It sits inside the field, then floats up into the border as you type, so you never forget what the box was for.

researchPlaceholder-only labels vanish as you type and strain memory; a label should stay visible. NN/g
tasteFloating it into the border is Google's way, which we like, with rounder corners and a real outline.

What's live, lives up top.

The Beacon is one black pill for the one thing happening now: a flight, a build, an agent at work. Tap it and it grows into a card; tap away and it folds back.

researchShow the status of what's going on, always, in the same place. Nielsen's first heuristic

Big enough for a thumb.

Buttons and tabs are at least 44 px on a phone; kids get 48. Dense desktop tools may go down to 34.

researchThe smaller and further a target, the slower and more error-prone it is to hit. Fitts 1954; WCAG 2.5.5

The more often it happens, the less it moves.

Panels settle, menus ease in, and anything you do many times a minute doesn't move at all.

researchLarge or constant motion makes some people dizzy; with "reduce motion" on, everything becomes a short fade. WCAG 2.3.3

Themes

One base. Any personality.

A theme is the topping, picked to suit the app: colour, type, corners, size and texture. Underneath, every theme is the same language.

TechGraphite · mono-forward · 34 px
KidsPlayground · round, roomy · 48 px
TravelAzulejo · paper, Fraunces · 40 px
MoneyLedger · tabular numbers · 40 px
A theme changes

Colours and the one accent, the type face, how round the corners are, how big things are to tap, how tight the spacing is, and the texture of the surfaces.

A theme never changes

How each part is built, how it presses and moves, the motion budget, and the reading rules: 4.5 to 1 for text, 3 to 1 for icons, never colour alone.
